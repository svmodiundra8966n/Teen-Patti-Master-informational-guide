What Is Teen Patti Master Gold?

Teen Patti Master Gold refers to a version of the popular Indian three-card game often marketed with higher table limits compared to standard Teen Patti Master tables. The core gameplay remains identical to classic Teen Patti, also known as 3 Patti or Indian Poker. Each player receives three cards. Classic Hand Rankings (Unchanged on Gold)

Hand rankings do not change between standard Master tables and Gold tables. The strongest hand is still Trail (three cards of the same rank). The ranking order from highest to lowest is:

  • Trail / Trio – Three cards of identical rank (example: A-A-A). This is the strongest possible hand.
  • Pure Sequence – Three consecutive cards of the same suit (example: K-Q-J of hearts). Also called a straight flush.
  • Sequence – Three consecutive cards of mixed suits (example: 9-8-7 of different suits). Also called a straight.
  • Colour / Flush – Three cards of the same suit that are not in sequence (example: A-J-6 of spades).
  • Pair – Two cards of the same rank plus one unmatched card (example: Q-Q-5).
  • High Card – No pair, no sequence, no flush. The highest individual card decides the winner (example: A-10-4).

Some table variants reverse the ranking order (Muflis / lowball) or introduce wild cards (Joker, AK47). These variants are not universal. Confirm the specific rules of the table you join. The automatic hand evaluation present in most digital versions removes the need to calculate rankings manually, but understanding them helps with decisions about when to raise or fold.

How a Round Works

A typical round begins with a boot (the mandatory opening stake that goes into the pot). Players then act in turn. A player may choose to remain blind or become seen by looking at their three cards. Blind players usually contribute half the stake that seen players must put in. This structure rewards the risk of playing without information.

On your turn you can:

  • Call / match the current stake
  • Raise the stake (within table limits)
  • Pack / fold and leave the round
  • Request a show when only two players remain (subject to table rules)

The round ends when only one player remains or when a show compares the hands. The player with the stronger ranking wins the pot. Because rounds are short, many players complete several hands in a few minutes. On higher-limit Gold tables the same structure applies; only the size of the boot and the maximum stake increase.
