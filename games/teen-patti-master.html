In the classic format a standard 52-card deck is used. Two to six players are typical. Cards are dealt face down. Betting proceeds with players choosing to stay blind or look. Looking normally doubles the cost of subsequent bets compared with blind players. A player may pack at any time. When only two players remain a show can be requested. How the Game Is Played – Blind, Seen, Chaal and Pack

After the deal each player decides whether to play blind or seen. Remaining blind in the early stages is usually cheaper because the stake for a blind player is lower. Experienced players often stay blind until the pot has grown, then look only when the cost of continuing becomes material. Looking immediately removes that cost advantage for the remainder of the hand. On each turn a player may match the current chaal, raise it, or pack. The term “chaal” refers to the current bet amount. A seen player must normally place a higher amount than a blind player. Exact multiples and the maximum number of blind rounds vary by table and by application. Most hands do not contain a strong combination. Trail and Pure Sequence appear infrequently, so folding weak high-card or weak-pair hands is often the better long-term decision. Bluffing is possible but constrained by the short hand size and visible betting patterns.

When a show occurs both remaining players reveal their cards. The higher-ranking hand takes the pot. Ties are uncommon because the six categories are well differentiated; when they do occur the higher individual card ranks decide the winner. Advanced players track the number of players still in the hand and the relative cost of continuing versus the strength of their own cards. Over many hands the advantage belongs to those who fold more often than they continue with marginal holdings.

Detailed Hand Strength and Common Examples

Trail, three cards of the same rank, is the strongest hand. Three Aces is the highest possible Trail and beats every other combination. Even the lowest Trail (three Twos) still defeats every Pure Sequence, Sequence, Colour, Pair and High Card. Pure Sequence consists of three consecutive cards of the same suit, such as King-Queen-Jack of hearts. Sequence is three consecutive cards of mixed suits. Colour is three cards of the same suit that do not form a sequence. Pair is two cards of the same rank plus one unrelated card. High Card is any hand that fits none of the above categories and is ranked by the highest single card, then the second, then the third.

In standard published rule sets Pure Sequence ranks above Sequence and both rank above Colour. A high Colour can still lose to a low Sequence. Pair ranks above High Card but loses to every other category. Because Trail is rare the practical contest on most hands is between Sequences, Colours, Pairs and High Cards. Players who internalise these relative strengths make faster and more accurate decisions about whether to continue or pack. Some variants reverse the order of certain sequences; the ranking table inside the app is the only reliable authority for that specific build.
