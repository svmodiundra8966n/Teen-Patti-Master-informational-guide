Teen Patti Master Hand Rankings Explained

Understanding hand rankings is the foundation of Teen Patti Master. Most of the decisions you make come down to knowing which hands beat which. This guide explains the standard six categories used in Teen Patti Master and most offline Teen Patti games.

Complete Ranking Order

RankHandHow It WorksStrong Example
1Trail / TrioThree cards of the exact same rankA-A-A
2Pure SequenceThree consecutive ranks, same suitK-Q-J suited
3SequenceThree consecutive ranks, mixed suits9-8-7
4Colour / FlushThree cards of one suit, not consecutiveA-J-6 same suit
5PairTwo cards of the same rank plus one otherQ-Q-5
6High CardNone of the above combinationsA-10-4

Trail (Three of a Kind)

Trail is the strongest hand in Teen Patti Master. Three Aces is the highest possible Trail. Three Twos is the lowest Trail but still beats every other category. Players who hold a Trail usually stay in the hand unless the pot has already grown very large and they have a specific reason to fold.

Pure Sequence vs Sequence

A Pure Sequence is three consecutive cards of the same suit. A regular Sequence is three consecutive cards of mixed suits. Pure Sequence always ranks higher than Sequence. This is one of the most common points of confusion for new Teen Patti Master players. Remembering that suited consecutive cards beat mixed consecutive cards prevents many unnecessary losses.

Colour, Pair and High Card

Colour (also called Flush) is three cards of the same suit that are not in sequence. It beats a Pair but loses to any Sequence. A Pair is two cards of the same rank. High Card is the weakest category and is decided by the highest card, then the second highest, and so on.

When two players have the same type of hand, the higher cards decide. For example, a Pair of Kings beats a Pair of Queens. An Ace-high hand beats a King-high hand when both are High Card.

Why Rankings Matter on Teen Patti Master Tables

Knowing Teen Patti Master card rankings helps you fold weak hands early and stay in with stronger ones. Players who confuse Pure Sequence with Colour or who overvalue a low Pair tend to lose chips faster. The same ranking order is used on Teen Patti Gold and Teen Patti VIP tables as well, so learning it once helps across versions.
